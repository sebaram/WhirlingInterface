Before you start
  • Webcam demos need camera permission and run best on a laptop or PC.
  • On mobile, use landscape orientation.
  • Move the viewpoint with WASD or the arrow keys.
  • Headset demos need a WebXR browser with hand tracking (Quest, Vision Pro, …).

Webcam demos — MediaPipe hand tracking

12 targets — museum gallery

Twelve works on a gallery wall, each with its own info marker. The paper's main configuration, put somewhere it would actually be used.

WebcamMediaPipeA-Frame

2 closely spaced targets

Two markers on a single artwork, side by side — the hardest case for ray-based pointing, and where motion matching pays off most.

WebcamMediaPipeA-Frame

12 targets — plain grid

The original layout with nothing around it: no environment, the original target sizes, and plain hand/joint dropdowns. Use this one to look at timing and correlation on their own.

WebcamMediaPipePlain

Every webcam demo takes a ?scene= parameter, or use the Scene dropdown in the controls panel: museum · outdoor · plain.

Headset demos — WebXR hand tracking Under development

The WebXR versions are still being worked on and may be incomplete or change without notice. The webcam demos above are the stable ones.

2 targets — WebXR only

The two-target scene driven by the headset's native hand tracking instead of a webcam. Enter immersive mode to try it.

Hand interaction sandbox

Pinch, press and grab primitives through a floating menu — a reference scene for the hand-tracking building blocks used by the demos above.

Hand skeleton viewer

Renders every tracked hand joint as a sphere in AR passthrough. Useful for checking what joint data a device actually reports.
